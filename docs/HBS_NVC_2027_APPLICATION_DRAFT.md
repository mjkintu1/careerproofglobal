## Short versions (for fields with tight limits)

**One line (about 25 words):** CareerProof Global shows African students and job seekers every route to any career, with official pay and admission rules for their country, free.

**Summary (about 100 words):** Millions of young Africans choose subjects, courses and first jobs with almost no reliable information: careers are invisible, admission rules are scattered in PDFs, and online career sites quote American pay. CareerProof Global is a free web platform that maps every route from school, or from any job, to any career, using official international data: the ILO's occupation standard, EU and US skills databases, national pay statistics and published admission rules. It is live, covers every country at a basic level and Uganda in depth, and earns from schools, universities and employers that want to reach or understand students.

## The problem

- **Careers are invisible.** Students know a handful of professions. Thousands of real occupations, and the routes into them, are never explained.
- **Subject choices close doors early and silently.** The wrong A-level combination at 16 can rule out medicine or engineering, and students often learn this only when they apply.
- **Admission rules are opaque and scattered.** Each country, and often each university, has its own rules; past cut-offs live in PDFs and on social media.
- **Online career information is not built for Africa.** Most career sites are American or European, quote US pay and ignore African exams, regulators and institutions.
- **Job seekers are exposed to fake offers.** Recruitment-fee scams are common enough that labour ministries (for example South Africa's) publish warnings; Uganda's ministry now publishes a register of licensed recruiters for jobs abroad.

## The solution

CareerProof Global is a free, mobile-friendly web platform that joins up what students and job seekers now look up separately: **careers, the routes into them, pay, subjects, qualifications and where to study.** It is built on international standards, so it works in every country at a basic level and goes deeper country by country.

### Built and live today

1. **Path planner.** A visual, node-based map of every route from school, or from any job, to any career, with no limit on the number of steps, shown forwards (start to goal) or backwards (goal to start). It searches a network of 938 careers and 6,682 links built from our career guides, O\*NET related occupations, skills shared in the EU's ESCO classification and each career's entry routes. Moves based only on shared skills are labelled as less certain.
2. **Official pay by country.** Pay figures come only from official publishers, each in its own currency and year: US Bureau of Labor Statistics and UK Office for National Statistics pay by occupation, Canadian and Australian government data, Uganda's and South Africa's public-service salary scales, Kenya's legal minimum wages, World Bank and United Nations salary scales, and ILO averages and minimum wages for 170 countries. Starting pay is shown only where an entry scale is published.
3. **Careers on the ILO standard.** 68 guided careers across 12 fields, plus 2,909 occupations from the EU's ESCO classification, linked to the ILO's ISCO-08 codes and to O\*NET.
4. **Subjects and curricula.** 32 school subjects linked to UNESCO's fields of study and to the careers that rely on them. Pages for Cambridge IGCSE and A Levels, Pearson Edexcel International, the IB, AP and the SAT, Uganda's UCE/UACE, Kenya's KCSE, WASSCE and South Africa's NSC, and how Uganda, Kenya, South Africa and Nigeria recognise foreign results.
5. **Admissions from official sources.** Uganda's official weighting system (Notes to Schools 2026/2027) in a points calculator, cut-offs for 113 programmes and all 3,629 active NCHE-accredited programmes; the UCAS Tariff calculator from UCAS's 2027 tables; minimum entry requirements for about 200 countries from UNESCO data. The calculators never predict a "chance".
6. **Where to study.** 30,326 universities and colleges in 223 countries, and each country's higher-education regulator.
7. **Career tools.** A CV and résumé builder that keeps everything on the user's own device, a salary checker that places a posted pay range against official pay for the same job and country, and a guide to finding work safely (official job services, recruitment-fee rules, scam warnings).
8. **Evidence on every page.** 72 data sources registered with their licences; every figure carries its source and date; anything unverified is labelled. FAQs answer common questions with a citation for each fact.

### Why it is distinctive

- **Routes, not lists.** Other sites describe careers one at a time; we show how to get from where you are to where you want to be, including career changes.
- **Official data only, cited.** No scraped salary estimates, no guessed admission odds. This is what schools, parents and funders can trust.
- **Africa first, global by design.** African exams, regulators, pay scales and institutions, on the same international codes national statistics offices use.

## Context

- **Open data has matured:** the ILO, the European Commission (ESCO), the World Bank and the US Department of Labor publish occupation and pay data under open licences; nobody has assembled it for African students.
- **Regulation:** we will handle data about minors. The live site needs no account, sets no cookies and uses no tracking; the CV builder stores data only on the user's device. Before accounts launch we will register with Uganda's Personal Data Protection Office and Kenya's ODPC, keep profiles minimal and record consent. **Why it is hard to copy:** the value is in the assembled, cited dataset (72 sources, each checked against its licence and access rules), the crosswalks between national and international codes, and country admission rules stored as versioned, tested data. Each new country adds to that lead.

## Risks and how we manage them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Wrong information misleads students | Official sources only; every fact carries its source and date; unverified items labelled; calculators only from official formulas |
| Admission data hard to collect | Partnerships with NCHE, PUJAB, KUCCPS and universities; "not yet published" shown instead of guesses |
| Data about minors | No personal data collected today; registration and consent before accounts |
| Licensing of third-party data | Only open-licensed or official sources, registered with their licences and attributions |
| Slow adoption | School partnerships; free, works on basic smartphones |
| Revenue takes time | Low running costs; start with schools and universities, where the value is clearest |
